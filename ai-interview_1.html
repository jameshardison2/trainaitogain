How the Mercor AI interview actually works.

Roughly twenty minutes, recorded, questions built from your résumé. Here's what to expect and where people lose points avoidably.

The format

  • Around 20 minutes, though some run shorter or longer
  • Video and audio recorded, with an AI-generated transcript
  • Questions are role-specific and generated from your résumé — expect to be asked about what's on it
  • Some are technical and go deep on prior experience; others assess language or reasoning
  • Some include a whiteboard, which saves automatically. Some require full-screen sharing, desktop only
Worth knowing

You complete each unique interview once. The result carries over automatically to other applications requiring the same one — so it isn't twenty minutes per application.

Practice first, because it's free

Mercor offers a Practice Interview that does not affect your future opportunities and is not shared with any company. If you've never done an AI interview, there's no reason to walk into the real one cold.

Setup mistakes that look like failure

A meaningful number of "stalled" applications are actually technical problems. From Mercor's own guidance:

  • Google Chrome on a desktop. Firefox and mobile browsers aren't supported
  • Test camera, mic, and speakers in the waiting room before starting
  • Quiet room, stable connection, headphones recommended
  • Full face visible, voice clear
  • Have pen, paper, and a calculator within reach

How to answer well

The evaluation rewards specific, evidence-backed answers over general claims.

  • Be concrete. "I fine-tuned a model for customer support classification using X" lands better than "I've worked with various models"
  • Lead with your conclusion, then support it with one number or example
  • Keep answers to roughly 45–90 seconds. Rambling gets cut off
  • Avoid long pauses — extended silence can prompt the AI to interject. If it cuts you off mid-thought, you can say you weren't finished
  • Don't overstate. The system cross-references what you say against your résumé. Claims you can't substantiate in detail work against you
Don't do this

Using ChatGPT or another LLM to compose answers during the interview is against Mercor's policy. Preparing beforehand is fine; generating answers live is not.

If your background is specialized

If your expertise is niche or crosses disciplines, questions can feel generic or slightly off-target. That's the system having less to pattern-match against, not a verdict on your experience.

  • Write a one-sentence plain-language version of what you do, and lead with it
  • Define your own terms as you use them — don't assume shared context
  • State each key point twice: plainly first, then with the technical term attached
  • If a follow-up feels off, briefly restate your point before answering it
